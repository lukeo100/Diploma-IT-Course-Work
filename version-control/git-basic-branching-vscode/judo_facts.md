# Judo Facts

## Quick overview

Judo is a modern Japanese martial art and Olympic combat sport founded by Jigoro Kano in 1882. The word *judo* is often translated as “the gentle way,” reflecting the idea of using balance, timing, and efficient movement rather than relying only on strength. 

In competition, the aim is to throw an opponent onto their back, hold them down with a pin, or force a submission using a choke or joint lock within the rules. 

Judo has been contested in the Olympic Games since 1964 for men, and women’s Olympic judo was added in 1992. 


## Common judo throws

Judo throwing techniques are called **nage-waza**. Traditional judo throws are commonly grouped into standing techniques and sacrifice techniques, with standing techniques further divided into hand, hip, and foot or leg techniques. 

### Beginner-friendly and widely used throws

- **Seoi-nage**: Shoulder throw. A very common forward throw where the attacker turns in and throws the opponent over the shoulder or back. 
- **O-soto-gari**: Major outer reap. A classic leg throw where the attacker reaps the opponent’s leg from the outside. 
- **O-goshi**: Major hip throw. A fundamental hip throw where the attacker uses the hip as a pivot point. 
- **Uchi-mata**: Inner thigh throw. A popular attacking throw that uses the leg to lift or sweep through the opponent’s inner thigh. 
- **Tai-otoshi**: Body drop. A hand technique where the attacker turns, blocks with the leg, and pulls the opponent forward and down. 
- **O-uchi-gari**: Major inner reap. A leg technique that attacks the opponent’s leg from the inside. 
- **Ko-uchi-gari**: Minor inner reap. A smaller inside leg reap often used as a setup or combination throw. 
- **De-ashi-harai**: Advancing foot sweep. A timing-based sweep used as the opponent steps forward. 
- **Harai-goshi**: Sweeping hip throw. A hip throw that combines hip rotation with a sweeping leg action. 
- **Tomoe-nage**: Circle throw. A sacrifice throw where the attacker drops backward and uses a foot to project the opponent over. 

## How throws are built

Most judo throws involve three key phases:

1. **Kuzushi**: Breaking the opponent’s balance. 
2. **Tsukuri**: Entering or fitting into position for the throw. 
3. **Kake**: Executing and completing the throw. 

## Where judo is most popular

Judo is practiced worldwide, and the International Judo Federation describes the sport as being present in more than 200 countries. citeturn1search18

Countries especially associated with strong judo cultures and international success include **Japan, France, South Korea, Brazil, Russia, Germany, the Netherlands, Georgia, Mongolia, and Cuba**. 

Japan is the birthplace of judo and has a deep cultural connection to the sport. 

France is one of the strongest judo countries outside Japan, with a large club network and a strong record in international competition. 

South Korea, Brazil, Russia, Germany, Georgia, and Mongolia are also frequently recognised for producing elite competitive judoka. 

## Interesting facts

- A judo practitioner is called a **judoka**. 
- The judo uniform is called a **judogi**. 
- Judo includes throwing techniques, pinning techniques, chokes, and joint locks, but strikes and weapons are not used in normal judo competition. 
- The Kodokan system recognises a large catalogue of official throwing techniques, and judo resources commonly list the Kodokan throwing syllabus as 67 throws. citeturn1search32turn1search40
- Judo influenced several other grappling and combat sports, including Brazilian jiu-jitsu and sambo. 

## Mini glossary

- **Dojo**: A training hall.
- **Judoka**: A person who practices judo. 
- **Judogi**: The judo uniform. 
- **Tatami**: The mat surface used for judo practice and competition.
- **Ippon**: A full-point score that can immediately win a judo match.
- **Nage-waza**: Throwing techniques. 
- **Katame-waza**: Grappling or control techniques, including holds, chokes, and joint locks.
